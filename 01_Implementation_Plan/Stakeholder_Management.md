## 3. Communication Plan

### 3.1 Communication Objectives

1. Ensure all stakeholders have timely, relevant information
2. Build and maintain support for the program
3. Manage expectations effectively
4. Facilitate informed decision-making
5. Create feedback loops for continuous improvement

### 3.2 Communication Channels

| Channel | Purpose | Audience | Frequency | Owner |
|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| **Steering Committee Briefings** | Strategic decisions, approvals | SC Members | Monthly | Program Manager |
| **Executive Dashboard** | Real-time KPIs | Leadership | Live | PMO |
| **Program Newsletter** | Progress updates | All stakeholders | Bi-weekly | Comms Lead |
| **Technical Working Groups** | Design decisions | Technical staff | Weekly | Tech Architect |
| **Stakeholder Portal** | Self-service information | Extended audience | Continuous | PMO |
| **Annual Report** | Comprehensive review | All stakeholders | Annually | Program Manager |
| **Crisis Communications** | Incident notifications | As needed | Real-time | Incident Commander |

### 3.3 Key Messages by Phase

#### Phase 1 (2026) - Foundation
> "Building the cornerstone of Algeria's cyber defense capability with world-class infrastructure and initial operational capacity."

#### Phase 2 (2027) - Expansion
> "Scaling our defenses to protect critical national assets with AI-powered detection and rapid response capabilities."

#### Phase 3 (2028) - Maturation
> "Achieving operational excellence with industry-leading detection and response times, protecting Algeria's digital future."

#### Phase 4 (2029) - Optimization
> "Optimizing and extending coverage to achieve comprehensive national cyber resilience."

#### Phase 5 (2030) - Leadership
> "Establishing Algeria as a regional leader in cybersecurity, capable of defending against nation-state threats."

## 4. Stakeholder Expectations Management

### 4.1 Common Expectations & Responses

| Expectation | Reality | Management Approach |
|-------------|---------|---------------------|
| "Immediate full protection" | Phased approach required | Clear roadmap communication, quick wins highlighting |
| "Zero incidents" | Risk reduction, not elimination | Risk-based metrics, industry comparisons |
| "Low cost" | Significant investment required | ROI analysis, cost of breach comparison |
| "Quick staffing" | Skills shortage globally | Training pipeline, competitive compensation |
| "Set and forget" | Continuous evolution required | Ongoing investment narrative, adaptation examples |

### 4.2 Managing Conflicting Interests

| Conflict | Parties Involved | Resolution Mechanism |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Budget vs Timeline | Finance vs Operations | Prioritized scope, phased funding |
| Security vs Usability | Security vs End Users | Risk-based exceptions, user training |
| Centralization vs Autonomy | National vs Sector SOCs | Hybrid model, clear delegation |
| Transparency vs Secrecy | Public vs Intelligence | Classification guidelines, sanitized reports |
| Speed vs Quality | PMO vs QA | Minimum viable quality gates, iterative improvement |

## 5. Issues & Escalation Management

### 5.1 Issue Categories

| Category | Examples | Typical Resolution Time | Escalation Path |
|----------|----------|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Resource** | Staff shortages, budget delays | 2-4 weeks | Program Manager → Steering Committee |
| **Technical** | Integration failures, bugs | 1-2 weeks | Tech Architect → Vendor |
| **Political** | Policy conflicts, priorities | 4-8 weeks | Program Manager → Minister |
| **Stakeholder** | Resistance, conflicts | 1-4 weeks | Program Manager → Sponsor |
| **External** | Vendor issues, supply chain | 2-6 weeks | Procurement → Legal |

### 5.2 Escalation Matrix

```
Level 1: Workstream Lead → Resolve within 48 hours
Level 2: Program Manager → Resolve within 1 week
Level 3: Steering Committee → Resolve within 1 month

```
